The road to recovery is long. Especially when you're having fun. After some
weeks, we've had time to rest, reflect and get back into our daily lives post
DEF CON. Which means, we owe you Nixies (can we coin this) our 2026
retrospective.

This was year two of the Nix Community at DEF CON. Everything that went wrong
in year one did not go as wrong this year, mostly because setup in the Las
Vegas Convention Center wasn't as rushed. And, well, mostly, due to supply
chain issues regarding the Nix Badge. But let's digress. Despite that and some
small A/V hiccups along the way (although we had a much better overall setup
from DEF CON this year), we were able to get things flowing. It was very much
smooth sailing considering it was no longer our first rodeo.

----

We are grateful to have had some of the same
[sponsors](https://nix.vegas/2026#sponsors) back on board this year as well as
new ones. Thanks to all of our sponsors for the support! You are a big reason
for why we are able to do the things we want to do, and we more than doubled
(~2.4x) last year's sponsorship. If you want to see where your money went,
scroll down!

Thanks also to our [speakers](https://nix.vegas/2026/speakers/) and attendees!
There was a lot of great content and interest within our space, which was about
double the size of last year (and we used *all* of it).

Lastly, we'd like to thank Dark Tangent and the Goon team for welcoming us back
and being incredibly pleasant to interact with all year.

----

Just like last year, our retrospective is structured by our requirements, which
were mostly to have a great time at DEF CON. In the spirit of open source,
we're sharing it openly.

# Theme

> "Smart people in bad structures produce trash. Ordinary people, well
> organized, can produce precious gems."
>
> \- Pieter Hintjens

This year's DEF CON theme was
[Agency](https://defcon.org/html/defcon-34/dc-34-theme.html). Our play on it
(with the lovely art of [Kenz Tobias](https://instagram.com/kenz_tobias_art))
was _Escape Your Fate_.

> Agency is self-determination. It's about making choices that increase yours,
> AND helping others to control theirs.

We're subjected to companies who try to form cartels to dominate the market,
spreading [existential fear and
doom](https://bcantrill.dtrace.org/2026/09/13/the-contagion-of-fear/) across
social media. Even in the NixOS community, there's been the rightful question
of keeping the community autonomous. Unchecked fear causes people working
toward the same goal to turn against each other, atomizing and weakening the
friendships and connections we make in the process.

We have evolved to interpret fear strongly. The fear of rejection, of being
attacked by others, of being shut out of doing the thing we love, are all
emotions that we know take priority over all the rest. Fear means danger. We
are sometimes subservient to it, and lose our way. If we can only address the
present danger, what higher purpose can we contribute to?

> [Pessimistic merging] gives maintainers power over new contributors,
> which many maintainers abuse.
>
> \- Pieter Hintjens, [_Social Architecture_](https://hintjens.gitbooks.io/social-architecture/content/)

The Nix Community at DEF CON is many things to many people, but _unafraid of your drama_ is
somewhere at the top of the list. We reject the notion that an open source
community should be allowed to be anything less than positive sum. When
maintainers form fiefdoms and cliques that make you pay a toll on your sanity
to smash that merge button, the enjoyment gets lost in the process.

Find your way <span title="google this phrase">out of the fog</span>. Let the forks flow freely, and maybe rediscover
your agency. The software was always free. You were too, regardless of what the flamewar on Github or Discourse told you.

# Bigger and better

We didn't want to get our own hopes up, and thought this year might be a little
smaller. Novelty wears off after the first time, people go and do other things.
But, apparently people want to do this crazy thing in the desert every year
with us and that means we're committed.

Our space was close to twice as large this year, so we _added_ a few things
compared to last year. The NixCTF, NixOS running on people's badges, and the
Nix Party were the highlights.

# Where's the money, Vegas?

While the [NixCTF](/blog/2026/ctf) and [badge](/blog/2026/badge) have their own
blogposts that you can explore in more detail, we want to answer the question
of where all the sponsor money went.

TL;DR: we had over double the sponsorship year over year. Vegas is not a cheap
place, so THANK YOU to [everyone who helped us this
year](https://nix.vegas/2026#sponsors). It wasn't bet all on black or on Starknet tokens.

![2025: $13,040 spent, $6,500 from sponsors, $6,540 out of pocket](/img/blog/2026-retrospective/sponsorship-2025.svg)

![2026: $16,055 spent, $15,615 from sponsors, roughly broke even](/img/blog/2026-retrospective/sponsorship-2026.svg)

- We were able to use all of Flox's Closure tier sponsorship to ensure our
attendees had DEF CON badges.
- Determinate Systems' Closure tier sponsorship helped fund a couple more
badges, [Kenz's art](/blog/2026/artwork), and a good chunk of the wonderful shirts we had made from [DCL Productions](https://www.dclproductions.com/).

helped direct people to our space very well.
- Nixcademy's Builder tier sponsorship helped us pay for our first Nix Party,
which was basically us renting out ~4 tables at
[Chayo](https://www.chayolv.com/) (and paying Vegas Mexican restaurant prices
for 20-25 people to have pitchers of frozen margaritas). This also helped with
incidentals like badge lanyards and some of the cost of our signage.

Next year, we will be trying to use our [OpenCollective](https://opencollective.com/NixVegas) more as it provides some useful tools.

## More hosting sponsorship

We use EDIS Global for our egress provider due to their proximity to Fastly.
Most of our VPS are sub-$15/month but we spin up a more powerful one for the
stream every year just for the event. This is expensive.

Hardware donations are still really useful. We're still using our Protectli and
Tenstorrent hardware, and ran our whole network off it. We'll be adding more
builders as we do more stuff.

We will assuredly do more stuff up until [DEF CON is canceled](http://defcon.ws/).

## Badge?

We're probably going to attempt an iteration of the badge every year (note the
question mark). PCBWay was helpful but we welcome more help.

Full disclosure, we've never really offered it as a sponsor option directly
because we think that's more of a risky investment than a sponsorship given
supply chain issues, but also maybe we should give ourselves more credit due to
the rabbit we pulled out of a hat this year.

Many of us admire the work of the #badgelife community, especially the work of
[AND!XOR](https://www.andnxor.com/) and [Hack For
Satan](https://hackforsatan.github.io/). While we're relatively new to the
scene, we think we can pull off plenty of neat ideas at this point. There is a
lot of concentrated knowledge in the team that modern tools only seem to be
helping.

# New things this year

There were also the minor vibe-enhancing things we did this year:

## CTF integrated with Home Assistant

We had a bunch of smart LED lamps wired into [Home
Assistant](https://www.home-assistant.io/). Whenever someone captured a flag in
the CTF, our CTF backend would hit a Home Assistant webhook on a private VLAN
and they'd all flash green.

We want to do even more of these lamps, and joked that we could run a Home
Assistant CTF in general with our setup. It may not be a joke at some point.

![The Nix Community
space](https://relive.nix.vegas/share/photo/6ZE3in8vcoko7EtG8PC2CkG7d3_eyc68VTgMnGrKVRYsaTh9L_3VlNxZseOKXC4wUc8/adfe1b34-a41d-43ea-935a-ee075766a28f/preview)

> The community space before being crowded with people

## Local live captioning

We ran a local Whisper speech model to do live captioning of talks. It was
super useful once we figured out how to hook it up to OBS, which also did our
sponsor logo rotation and Grafana dashboard, but that Tenstorrent box won't
clock down and gets really hot. We need better thermal management for it when
we aren't using the cards (i.e. to turn them off).

## More cached packages

We also had a Hydra and local Forgejo server that we're still using, and
planning on making available to attendees to use in the off days. At least one
of our CTF challenges used the git server, and we're planning on putting our
[BadgeOS](https://git.nixos.lv/NixVegas/BadgeOS) build on our Hydra.

We expanded our cached package set to all stable and unstable versions, and
even the last release of 25.11. All of our CTF VMs were able to use it.

## Alternate upstreams than DC Wifi

We tried to keep our uplink alive with a [Calyx
Institute](https://calyxinstitute.org/) modem even when DEF CON's was down,
and offered a [flake](https://github.com/NixVegas/dcwifi) for automatic
enrollment of people's laptops to the DEF CON Wifi registration.

We want to do even better with bonding next year. Someone should be able to
come to the Nix Community and we should always have working internet access.

We figured out the mixer on Saturday, and wired it up so we could stream
[Tormented Radio](https://tormentedradio.com/) back into people's headsets
while they worked. Naturally, we need to just do our own radio station now,
with a local Jellyfin server so we aren't dependent on the uplink as much. It's
something that's been on our radar for a while in any case.

## Better PXE boot

We could PXE boot machines into a system that would auto-register on DEF CON
wifi. That was a neat trick. Ideally we'll have a proper WPA Enterprise network
next year :)

Our PXE boot could also, naturally, make the machine play Bad Apple because we
figured out how to sleep for a fractional number of seconds in iPXE's scripting
language. We also hid a CTF flag so people had to either download the iPXE
script or just boot into it and watch all of Bad Apple before the flag printed.

# Other events

We probably should do more with the Projects track (notably, infra integration)
and not do two PR merging sessions. We got through like 10 PRs on Friday and
then it tapered off on Saturday. This was somewhat expected but we should vary
it a little. Remote participation worked nicely through Owncast. We'd probably
bridge Matrix, Discord, and Owncast in the future so we just have a channel
full of whatever people are chatting on.

And, regardless, have two talk submission deadlines: one for DEF CON's
Community Stage deadline and the other for our own. That would unconfuse things
so we don't need to extend our deadline for a third year.

# Conclusion

We had a little [challenge for the community](/blog/2025/2025-retrospective/#conclusion) last year:

> For next year, here's a hint: Nix fixes everything. What can you fix with it?
> - What's the weirdest machine you can get a Nix-based Linux distro running
>   on?
> - Ever thought about "nontraditional" execution environments for Linux
>   packages, or maybe Nix evaluation itself?
> - Does the idea of finding a sandbox escape in Nix builds fill you with
>   excitement?

All three of these were addressed by the badge, the CTF, and various talks in
different ways. [Weird machines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weird_machine) are our strong suit! And, in a funny
coincidence, [@psyclyx/fix](https://github.com/psyclyx/fix) on a badge
controlling the LEDs _was_ the nontraditional environment for Nix evaluation.

Fate status: escaped ✅ (along with multiple Nix sandboxes and the SG200x's
secure boot)
